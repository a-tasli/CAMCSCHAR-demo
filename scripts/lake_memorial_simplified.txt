[1] We are now standing by the large artificial lake, which is the beautiful nature and water center of the Sabancı University campus.
[2] This huge lake was cleverly made by filling the deepest hole left behind by old gravel mining before the school was built.
[3] Builders carefully flattened the bottom, sealed it with special clay so water wouldn't leak, and added gravel to make a healthy environment for water life.
[4] To fill and keep the lake full, a very smart and closed water recycling system was built all around the campus.
[5] Rainwater is collected from the big roofs of the school buildings and dorms, and then it is sent straight into the lake through many pipes.
[6] Importantly, the lake also holds the campus's cleaned wastewater, making it a very green and eco-friendly recycling system.
[7] Because of this great system, even during very dry years, the lake has enough water to keep all the grass and plants on campus green.
[8] The lake is so clean and healthy that wild animals and fish started living in it naturally, with seagulls bringing fish eggs from the sea.
[9] Around the peaceful lake is the school's famous collection of Japanese Cherry, or Sakura, trees, which is the biggest collection in Turkey.
[10] Between 2007 and 2009, thanks to a generous gift from a Japanese company named Komatsu, 2,500 young and fragile Sakura trees were brought to Turkey.
[11] With the hard work of Japanese plant experts over many years, almost 2,000 trees grew strong and are now over four meters tall.
[12] This amazing group of trees includes seven pure types that cover the campus in bright pink and white colors every spring, just like in Japan.
[13] Next to these beautiful trees is the Sakura Stone Monument, a giant stone weighing 3.3 tons that is about 10,000 years old.
[14] This perfectly smooth and round stone was taken from a river in central Japan, shaped over thousands of years by the flowing water.
[15] The stone was chosen to show strength and toughness, representing the long-lasting friendship between the university and its friends in Japan.
[16] The stone has the Japanese word for "Sakura" carved into it, drawn beautifully by a former leader of the Japanese company.
[17] Together, the smart lake, the blooming trees, and the old stone make a very peaceful and touching place, perfect for taking photos during graduation day.
[18] The smart design of this man-made lake also helps cool down the campus air during the very hot summer days.
[19] Visitors can often watch many different types of birds that have chosen to live safely in this healthy area.
[20] Planting the Sakura trees next to the water was not just for looks; it was a special choice to share culture and plants with other countries.
[21] Every spring, the beautiful blooming flowers bring students, artists, and nature lovers from all over to see this short but amazing event.
[22] The Gujyo stone monument stands strong among the soft, falling petals, showing a beautiful difference between solid rock and delicate flowers.
[23] This whole part of the campus proves that when people build things with care, they can fix and improve nature.
